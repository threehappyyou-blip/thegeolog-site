- site: warminsight.com / loosecopy.com
- date noticed: 2026-09-02
- what happened: automated pipeline drafts casual Reddit/Quora posts from warminsight newsletter content, aimed at getting insights in front of relevant communities. Tried posting one about bond funds and interest rates to r/povertyfinance and r/UKPersonalFinance. Reddit flagged it: "This looks like it may have been written by AI."
- why it actually got flagged: not really a "prose sounds robotic" problem. the post was written in a casual first-person voice ("had a wild realization about my 401k...") ending with a link back to the site framed like an organic recommendation ("found this breakdown here, if u care") — no disclosure that it was AI-drafted or came from the site's own operator. subreddits with explicit AI-content rules have more than doubled since 2023, and Reddit's own self-promotion norm caps self-promotional activity at roughly 10% of total activity, with the rest expected to be genuine participation.
- root cause: the pipeline was built to fully automate posting undisclosed, semi-anonymous-sounding content pointing back to a commercial site. that pattern was always going to collide with the self-promo rule and the disclosure requirement, independent of how human the prose reads.
- decision: instead of trying to prompt-engineer around detection, started building a separate tool — a content repurposing assistant that stops at the draft. paste a blog post or notes, get a Twitter thread + LinkedIn post draft, but a human finishes it, discloses AI use if needed, and posts it themselves. the AI's job ends at the draft.
- status: landing page live at loosecopy.com, waitlist open. no backend or real product logic built yet — deliberately validating demand before building further.
