Ask how much AI Overviews have cut organic click-through rates and you'll get
a confident-sounding answer no matter who you ask. The trouble is that the
confident answers don't agree with each other. I pulled the four most
methodologically serious studies I could find rather than picking whichever
number made the best headline, and laid them out side by side.

## Four studies, four different numbers

**A pre-registered field experiment.** Researchers recruited participants,
split them into groups, and used a browser extension to hide AI Overviews in
real time for one group while leaving search normal for another. This is
about as close to a controlled trial as search-behavior research gets. Over
two weeks, removing AI Overviews raised outbound clicks per search from 0.38
to 0.61 — meaning their presence was suppressing organic clicks on affected
queries by roughly 38%, with zero-click search rising from 54% to 72% when
they were shown.

**Pew Research Center.** Analyzing close to 69,000 real Google searches, Pew
found people clicked through to a traditional result about 8% of the time
when an AI Overview appeared, versus 15% when it didn't — roughly half the
click rate, measured from actual browsing data rather than estimated from
keyword tools.

**Seer Interactive.** Tracking over 5 million queries and more than 2 billion
organic impressions across dozens of brands from January 2025 through early
2026, Seer found organic click-through on AI-Overview-present queries fell
from 1.76% to a low of 0.61% by September 2025 — a 65% relative decline —
before climbing back to around 2.4% by February 2026.

**Ahrefs.** Looking specifically at the #1 organic ranking position, Ahrefs
found AI Overviews cut its click rate by 34.5% in April 2025, widening to 58%
by December 2025 — a result they describe as still trending in one direction
rather than leveling off.

## Why the numbers won't converge

These aren't measuring the same thing. Different studies cover different
query types, different date ranges, different countries, and different
definitions of what counts as an "AI Overview query" in the first place —
informational queries lose far more clicks than transactional ones, and the
mix of query types in any sample shifts the average. On top of that, the
underlying behavior is still moving: Seer's own numbers show a partial
recovery in early 2026 after the steepest drop in late 2025, which means any
single snapshot is already somewhat dated by the time it's published. Treat
the range — roughly 38% to 65% — as the honest answer, not any one figure
inside it.

## The reframe that actually matters more than the exact percentage

Buried in the same research is a number that matters more for deciding what
to do next: brands that get cited inside an AI Overview see roughly 35% more
organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks than brands that don't, on the same
kinds of queries. The click isn't disappearing so much as it's being
rerouted — toward whichever handful of sources the AI Overview decided to
name. Losing the click on a query where a competitor gets cited and gaining
it back on a query where you do are the same underlying mechanism, just
pointed in opposite directions.

## The takeaway

None of the four studies here are wrong, and none of them should be quoted
as if they're the definitive number — they're measuring a moving target from
four different angles. What they agree on, regardless of the exact
percentage: fewer clicks are happening on the page overall, and citation
inside the AI-generated answer is doing more of the work that ranking used to
do. That's the whole argument for why AI search visibility is worth the
effort in the first place, and it's a more useful takeaway than memorizing
any single statistic in this post.
